# Evidence and output contract

## Evidence labels

Treat claims as one of:

- `Observed`: directly visible in a supplied source.
- `Estimated`: supplied by a licensed third-party dataset such as Semrush.
- `Inferred`: a marketing hypothesis that must be tested on the call.
- `Private-data-required`: requires analytics, Search Console, an ad account,
  CRM, revenue data or founder confirmation.

Use descriptive Markdown links such as `[Company homepage](https://example.com)`.
Do not paste bare URLs throughout the document or dump raw evidence JSON.

## Required report order

Use these exact H2 headings:

1. Founder & Company Background
2. Executive Marketing Brief
3. Website & Conversion Review
4. SEO & Search Visibility
5. Traffic & Channel Intelligence
6. Paid Media & Creative Signals
7. Social, Email & Technology
8. Competitor Landscape
9. 10 Positives
10. 10 Growth Gaps
11. 5 Discovery Questions
12. Recommended Call Agenda
13. Data Boundaries & Access Needed
14. Sources

Start with the founder and company context, including the supplied agenda. In
the website section cover positioning, offer, ICP, CTA hierarchy, conversion
journey, proof, lead capture, mobile UX and speed. In SEO, clearly separate
technical SEO, on-page/content, organic visibility and Semrush estimates.

Every positive and gap must contain a short title, why it matters, descriptive
evidence citation and confidence. Use exactly 10 positives, 10 gaps and 5
questions, with one numbered line per item and no nested lists.

Use tables for structured comparisons. Use bold only for short finding titles
or labels. Keep the report readable in under ten minutes.

Use `not observed in this bounded check` instead of `inactive`. A pixel is a
technology observation, never proof of active ads, retargeting, spend,
performance or creative fatigue. Semrush metrics are estimates, not the
founder's private analytics.
